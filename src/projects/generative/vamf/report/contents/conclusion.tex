\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec: conclusion}

We introduced the \textbf{curvature-variance principle}, a unifying theoretical lens that attributes the three independently reported MeanFlow training pathologies---gradient conflict~\cite{zhang2025alphaflowunderstandingimprovingmeanflow}, Jacobian amplification~\cite{geng2025improvedmeanflowschallenges}, and the curvature bottleneck~\cite{zhang2026overcomingcurvaturebottleneckmeanflow}---to a single quantity: the material derivative of the marginal velocity field. The principle yields three design directives, which we instantiate as \textbf{Variance-Aware MeanFlow (VaMF)}---an EMA velocity anchor (\Cref{subsubsec: v-anchor}) that eliminates the Jacobian-amplified gradient noise, a flow-matching anchor (\Cref{subsubsec: va-obj}) that supervises the boundary condition without an additional head, and a closed-form Hutchinson-estimated trace weight (\Cref{subsubsec: trace-weight}) that approximates the per-sample BLUE under isotropic conditional-velocity noise. VaMF preserves the original MeanFlow MSE objective and backbone, adds a single Hutchinson JVP per step, and consistently improves over vanilla MeanFlow on every dataset of our 2-D toy benchmark under both SW$_1$ and SW$_2$ (mean SW$_1$ improvement $-28\%$ across the four datasets) and dominates from $d{=}4$ to $d{=}64$ on the dense-Gaussian-mixture scaling sweep; \Cref{appx: dit-results} additionally shows the same mechanism transfers to DiT-B/4 / ImageNet-$256$ latent training at a $\sim\!22\%$ wall-clock overhead.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The trace-weight derivation assumes isotropic conditional-velocity noise $\Sigma_{\vv'}\!\approx\!\sigma_{t}^{2}\mI_{d}$; distributions with strongly correlated $\Sigma_{\vv'}$ may benefit from a low-rank Jacobian factorization that we leave to future work. Our DiT-B/4 evaluation is single-seed at the time of submission; multi-seed FID confirmation will be added in the camera-ready version once the run completes.

\paragraph{Future work.} Two natural extensions stand out. \textit{(i) Anisotropic trace weights} learned from a low-rank approximation of $\Sigma_{\vv'}$ would tighten the BLUE bound at the cost of a small auxiliary head---bridging the closed-form trace weight of this paper with the per-pixel variance head sketched in concurrent work. \textit{(ii) Schedule design.} Our \textsc{blue-gauss} schedule for $\sigma_{t}$ is principled only under Laplace's approximation of $\pdata$; more general data distributions may benefit from a learned or moment-matched alternative that directly tracks $\Tr(\Sigma_{\vv'})/d$.
