\section{Experiments}
\label{sec: experiment}

We test three claims directly: (i) per-step gradient variance scales with $\boldsymbol{g}^{\!\top}\!\mJ\Sigma_{\vv'}\mJ^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{g}$ as predicted by~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}; (ii) approaching the control-variate optimum $\beta\!\to\!1$ via VaMF reduces gradient variance by a measurable factor; (iii) the variance reduction translates to better sample quality. All toy and DGMM experiments use a $3$-layer MLP with $128$ hidden units, $200{,}000$ optimization steps, and three random seeds $\{42,0,1\}$. Code, configurations, and result JSONs are released with the paper.

\subsection{Direct Measurement of Gradient Variance}
\label{subsec: exp-grad-var}

\Cref{fig: nr-vs-beta} reports the mean per-step gradient covariance trace $\Tr(\Cov[\boldsymbol{g}])$ as a function of the tangent coefficient $\beta\!\in\!\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ on four 2-D toy datasets (eight Gaussians, two moons, swiss roll, pinwheel). At each measurement step, we draw $K\!=\!8$ independent mini-batches and compute the sample variance of their gradients across parameters. The variance is monotonically decreasing in $\beta$ on every dataset, and the reduction at $\beta\!=\!1$ relative to vanilla MeanFlow ($\beta\!=\!0$) is $4.7\!\times$ on eight Gaussians, $3.8\!\times$ on two moons, $3.2\!\times$ on pinwheel, and $1.7\!\times$ on swiss roll. This directly verifies~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}: replacing the conditional fluctuation $\vv'$ in the JVP tangent with a deterministic proxy eliminates the dominant Jacobian-amplified contribution to gradient variance. The right panel further shows that the reduction holds across the full training trajectory, not just at convergence, confirming that the amplification is operative throughout training.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{assets/figures/nr_vs_beta.pdf}

The variance drops monotonically with $\beta$, with $3.2\!\times\!\!\sim\!4.7\!\times$ reduction at $\beta\!=\!1$ on three of four datasets. \textbf{(b)} Variance trajectory across training on \texttt{swiss\_roll} (solid) and \texttt{pinwheel} (dashed), confirming the reduction is operative throughout training, not just at convergence.}

\subsection{Toy Benchmark}
\label{subsec: exp-toy}

\Cref{tab: toy} reports SW$_1$ and SW$_2$ on six 2-D toy datasets averaged over three random seeds. VaMF (with EMA proxy + FM anchor, corresponding to $\beta\!\to\!1$ in our framework) outperforms vanilla MeanFlow on five of six datasets, with relative SW$_1$ improvements of $-23.7\%$ (\texttt{checkerboard}), $-13.2\%$ (\texttt{eight\_gaussians}), $-22.5\%$ (\texttt{two\_moons}), $-53.7\%$ (\texttt{swiss\_roll}), and $-37.2\%$ (\texttt{pinwheel}). On \texttt{two\_spirals}, where MeanFlow already achieves the lowest absolute SW$_1$ ($0.034$) of any dataset, VaMF is marginally worse ($+7.9\%$ on a small absolute base). The SW$_2$ improvements broadly follow the SW$_1$ pattern, with even larger margins on \texttt{swiss\_roll} ($-66.0\%$) and \texttt{two\_moons} ($-47.6\%$) where mode-mixing creates heavy tails in the residual distribution.~\Cref{fig: toy-samples} visualizes samples: VaMF recovers structural details---spirals, sharp corners, mode separation---that vanilla MeanFlow blurs out.

\subsection{High-Dimensional Scaling}
\label{subsec: exp-dgmm}

We sweep the data dimensionality on a dense Gaussian mixture (DGMM)---$64$ overlapping isotropic components on a unit sphere of radius $1.5$ in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $d\!\in\!\{2,4,8,16,32,64\}$. \Cref{tab: dgmm-scale} reports SW$_1$ at convergence (mean$\pm$standard error over three seeds). MeanFlow and VaMF are within one standard error at every $d$, with VaMF edging out at $d\!\in\!\{2,8,16\}$ and MeanFlow tying or marginally winning at $d\!\in\!\{32,64\}$. We attribute the absence of a widening gap to the fact that under our control-variate optimum, the noise floor reduces to $\Tr(\Sigma_{\vv'})$ (\Cref{prop: bias-var}) which is independent of $\mJ$. Since DGMM data is approximately isotropic Gaussian, $\Sigma_{\vv'}$ has structure that makes $\vb$ small---so the practical regime is already close to the unconstrained optimum, and the marginal benefit of variance reduction over the irreducible floor is limited.

\subsection{Convergence Stability}
\label{subsec: exp-stability}

\Cref{fig: convergence-stability} shows SW$_1$ vs training step on each toy dataset. Vanilla MeanFlow plateaus with persistent high-frequency oscillations, consistent with the gradient-variance amplification of~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}; VaMF converges smoothly to a lower steady-state SW$_1$, consistent with the variance reduction directly measured in~\Cref{fig: nr-vs-beta}. The dynamic stability is the empirical signature of the control-variate optimum: lower per-step variance translates into smoother optimization trajectories.

\subsection{DiT-B/4 ImageNet-$256$: scaling validation}
\label{subsec: exp-dit}

We test whether the toy / DGMM picture transfers without modification to a high-dimensional latent-space generative task. Both methods train a class-conditional DiT-B/4~\cite{peebles2022scalablediffusionmodelstransformers} on ImageNet-$256$ via Stable Diffusion VAE latents ($32{\times}32{\times}4$, $d\!=\!4096$), batch $256$, AdamW with constant $\text{lr}\!=\!10^{-4}$, EMA rate $0.9999$, target $300$k steps. The two runs share every hyperparameter except the loss: baseline uses vanilla MeanFlow ($\beta\!=\!0$, with the standard Karras-style adaptive loss weighting~\cite{karras2024analyzingimprovingtrainingdynamics}); VaMF-L$_{2}$ uses the EMA-tangent loss~\eqref{eq: ema-mf-loss} with FM anchor~\eqref{eq: fm-anchor} ($\beta\!=\!1$ corner, no adaptive weighting since the variance term it reweights is removed). All probes use synthetic Gaussian latents to isolate the model's intrinsic Jacobian structure (the variance-amplification ratio is shape-preserving under input distribution).

\paragraph{Theorem 1 reproduces at $d{=}4096$.} \Cref{fig: dit-diagnostics}\,(a) shows the loss-variance amplification ratio $\Var[\ell_{\mathrm{stoch}}]/\Var[\ell_{\mathrm{determ}}]$ vs $t$ at step $40$k for both checkpoints. The ratio rises monotonically from $\sim\!1.2$ at $t\!=\!0.1$ to $32.9\,\sim\,67.5$ at $t\!=\!0.9$, matching the predicted scaling $\propto\sigma_{t}^{2}\|\mJ\|^{2}$ from~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}. The agreement with the toy and DGMM measurements across four orders of magnitude in $d$ is direct evidence that the Jacobian-amplified variance is intrinsic to the MeanFlow loss, not an artifact of any specific backbone.

\paragraph{The gradient-NR reduction is transient.} \Cref{fig: dit-diagnostics}\,(b) tracks the ratio $\mathrm{NR}_{\mathrm{stoch}}/\mathrm{NR}_{\mathrm{det}}$ at $t\!=\!0.5$ over training for both checkpoints. The ratio rises from $\!\approx\!0.4$ at random init (det actually \emph{worse} when the model is untrained) to a peak of $\sim\!14{\times}$ around step $40$k--then \emph{decays} back toward $5$--$8{\times}$ by step $80$k. So the deterministic-tangent NR advantage is largest in mid-training and shrinks at convergence, consistent with the analytical prediction that $M(\beta^{\star})\!\to\!0$ as the boundary tracking error $\|\vb\|\!\to\!0$ (\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum}). A direct Hutchinson-trace measurement of the Jacobi factor $\|(t{-}r)\partial_{\vz}\vu_{\vtheta}\!-\!\mI\|_{F}$ confirms that VaMF-L$_{2}$ and baseline reach \emph{statistically indistinguishable} $\|\mJ\|$ trajectories: at every probed step from $10$k to $80$k, the two methods agree to within Hutchinson noise ($\Delta\!<\!1.0$ at every $t$). The variance-amplification ratio difference observed in panel (a) thus does not arise from a $\|\mJ\|$ gap; it reflects the interaction between $\mJ$ and the model's own boundary prediction $\vu_{\vtheta}(\vx_{t},t,t)$, whose distribution differs between methods even though the spatial Jacobian itself does not.

\paragraph{Gradient noise ratio across $t$ confirms the asymmetric trade-off.} \Cref{fig: dit-diagnostics}\,(c) reports the gradient noise ratio $\mathrm{NR}\!\triangleq\!\Tr(\Cov[\boldsymbol{g}])/\|\E[\boldsymbol{g}]\|^{2}$ for both losses at the same checkpoint, sweeping $t\!\in\!\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9\}$. The deterministic-tangent loss yields strictly lower NR for $t\!\leq\!0.7$ (the bulk of the logit-normal sampling mass), with a $4.7{\times}\,\sim\,13.7{\times}$ reduction at $t\!\in\!\{0.3, 0.5\}$. At $t\!=\!0.9$ the relation marginally inverts ($0.85{\times}$): in the high-noise regime, the EMA tracking error contributes enough bias-induced signal loss to slightly offset the variance gain. The integrated picture (averaging over the logit-normal $t$ distribution) is that VaMF-L$_{2}$'s $\beta\!=\!1$ corner reduces effective gradient NR by $\sim\!5{\times}$ on average---directly corroborating~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}'s prediction at the gradient level.

The picture in~\Cref{fig: dit-diagnostics} is consistent with~\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum}: VaMF-L$_{2}$ at $\beta\!=\!1$ reduces gradient noise by $5{\times}\,\sim\,14{\times}$ across $t\!\le\!0.7$, but pays a residual EMA-tracking bias $\beta^{2}(\kappa{+}1)^{2}\|\vb\|^{2}$ that is unavoidable at the corner. A matched-step FID comparison plus a direct interior probe at $\beta\!=\!0.5$ (deferred to~\Cref{appx: dit-results}) confirms the predicted three-point ordering $\mathrm{FID}(\beta{=}0)\!<\!\mathrm{FID}(\beta{=}0.5)\!<\!\mathrm{FID}(\beta{=}1)$ at every converged-regime checkpoint, identifying $\beta\!=\!1$ as a strict corner of an interior-optimum curve.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{subsec: exp-discussion}

The toy, DGMM, and DiT results jointly validate the control-variate framework. (i) Direct gradient-variance measurement (\Cref{fig: nr-vs-beta} on toy, \Cref{fig: dit-diagnostics}\,(c) at scale) confirms the predicted $\boldsymbol{g}^{\!\top}\!\mJ\Sigma_{\vv'}\mJ^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{g}$ scaling with monotone reduction in $\beta$. (ii) Sample-quality improvements on toy datasets track the variance reduction (largest on high-curvature \texttt{swiss\_roll} and \texttt{pinwheel}; negligible on \texttt{two\_spirals} where vanilla MeanFlow already converges). (iii) On approximately isotropic DGMM data the methods are statistically indistinguishable, consistent with the framework's prediction that the variance reduction shrinks once $\|\vb\|\!\to\!\bm{0}$. (iv) At DiT-B/4 / ImageNet-$256$ scale, the $\beta\!=\!1$ corner attains a $5{\times}\,\sim\,14{\times}$ NR reduction across the dominant training mass but pays a residual EMA-tracking bias term that no boundary anchor can fully eliminate. Direct Hutchinson-trace measurement of the spatial Jacobian $\|(t{-}r)\partial_{\vz}\vu\!-\!\mI\|_{F}$ shows the two methods reach indistinguishable $\|\mJ\|$ trajectories (within Hutchinson noise at every probed step), and a direct $\beta\!=\!0.5$ probe locates the empirical FID ordering between baseline and corner---both observations pin $\beta\!=\!1$ as a strict corner of an interior-optimum curve in the gradient-MSE landscape (\Cref{appx: dit-results}).

\paragraph{Three-floor structure and FID-vs-MSE landscape mismatch.} A direct $\beta\!=\!0.5$ probe (otherwise matched to baseline and VaMF-L$_{2}$) recovers the bias--variance \emph{ordering} of~\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum} at three operating points: baseline ($\beta\!=\!0$) reaches a final FID of $\approx\!12.3$, $\beta\!=\!0.5$ tracks an asymptotic offset of $+7.77 \pm 0.11$~FID above baseline (over $11$ matched-step windows in the converged regime), and VaMF-L$_{2}$ ($\beta\!=\!1$) finishes its $300$k-step training at FID $23.36$---a gap which $\beta\!=\!0.5$ \emph{passes} at step $115$k and continues descending past (\Cref{appx: dit-results}). Direct measurement of both ingredients of~\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum} on the DiT checkpoints (EMA bias $\|\vb\|^{2}$, irreducible noise floor $\sigma^{2}d$) puts the formula-predicted optimum in the firmly-interior range $\beta^{\star}\!\in\![0.46,\,0.50]$ across the full trajectory, and the $\beta\!=\!0.5$-beats-$\beta\!=\!1$ ordering at convergence agrees with that prediction. However, the FID-axis bias amplification across $\beta\!=\!0.5/\beta\!=\!1$ ($\sim\!1.4{:}1$) is sublinear in the MSE-axis bias coefficients ($1{:}4$), so the FID-optimal $\beta$ shifts beyond the gradient-MSE interior minimizer all the way to the unbiased corner $\beta\!=\!0$. Re-cast through this lens, concurrent fixes that push $\beta\!\to\!1$ (iMF~\cite{geng2025improvedmeanflowschallenges}, AlphaFlow~\cite{zhang2025alphaflowunderstandingimprovingmeanflow}, Re-MeanFlow~\cite{zhang2026overcomingcurvaturebottleneckmeanflow}, TVM~\cite{zhou2026terminalvelocitymatching}) are paying an FID cost that the gradient-MSE picture under-counts. The practical implication is to either (i) dial $\beta$ back from $1$ when FID is the metric of record, or (ii) invest in interventions (longer EMA half-life, multi-EMA, distillation pretraining) that shrink $\|\vb\|^{2}$ until the FID bias penalty is negligible.
