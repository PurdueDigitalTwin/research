\section{Experiments}
\label{sec: experiment}

We evaluate VaMF on three fronts: \textbf{(i)} a 2-D toy benchmark spanning four standard distributions, where we measure both sliced Wasserstein-$1$ and the variance-amplification diagnostics that motivate the trace weight; \textbf{(ii)} a high-dimensional dense-Gaussian-mixture (DGMM) scaling study in which we sweep the data dimensionality from $d{=}2$ up to $d{=}64$; and \textbf{(iii)} a class-conditional latent-space training of DiT-B/4 on ImageNet-256$\times$256 against the matched baseline MeanFlow recipe of~\cite{geng2025meanflowsonestepgenerative}. \Cref{subsec: exp-ablation} ablates the trace-weight component and the schedule $\sigma_t$ in~\eqref{eq: trace-weight}. All toy and DGMM experiments use a $3$-layer MLP with $128$ hidden units, $200{,}000$ optimization steps, and three random seeds $\{42,0,1\}$; the DiT experiment uses a single seed and $300{,}000$ steps, matching the official recipe of~\cite{geng2025meanflowsonestepgenerative}. Code, configurations, and result JSONs are released with the paper.

\subsection{Variance Amplification and the Trace Term}
\label{subsec: exp-diagnostic}

We first probe the per-sample loss variance and Jacobian-deviation norm on a frozen checkpoint trained on the eight-Gaussians dataset. \Cref{fig: teaser} plots the variance ratio $\Var[\gL_{\text{stoch}}]/\Var[\gL_{\text{det}}]$ as a function of $t$ (panel a) and, on a shared $t$-axis, the per-sample loss variance $\Var[\gL_{\text{det}}]$ alongside the Frobenius norm $\|(t{-}r)J - I\|_F$ that drives the amplification (panel b). The variance ratio peaks at $\sim\!259\!\times$ near $t\!\approx\!0.4$, in direct empirical agreement with~\cref{thm: jacobi-variance}; the per-sample loss variance does not vanish anywhere on $t\!\in\![0,1]$ and its scale tracks $\|(t{-}r)J - I\|_F$ across the integration window, identifying the Jacobi factor as the immediate source of the amplification.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{assets/figures/teaser.pdf}
    \caption{Variance amplification in vanilla MeanFlow. \textbf{(a)} The stochastic-vs-deterministic variance ratio $\Var[\gL_{\text{stoch}}]/\Var[\gL_{\text{det}}]$ peaks at $\sim\!259\!\times$ near $t\!\approx\!0.4$, in direct empirical agreement with~\cref{thm: jacobi-variance}. \textbf{(b)} The per-sample loss variance $\Var[\gL_{\text{det}}]$ (left axis) is non-vanishing across $t$ and co-varies with the Jacobi-factor norm $\|(t{-}r)J - I\|_F$ (right axis): both quantities peak in the same mid-$t$ regime, empirically certifying that the Frobenius norm of $(t{-}r)J - I$ governs the per-sample loss scale.}
    \label{fig: teaser}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Toy Benchmark}
\label{subsec: exp-toy}

\Cref{tab: toy} reports both SW$_1$ and SW$_2$ on the four 2-D toy datasets, averaged over three random seeds. VaMF-TW achieves the best score on every dataset under both metrics, with relative SW$_1$ improvements of $-23.4\%$ on \texttt{checkerboard}, $-13.3\%$ on \texttt{eight\_gaussians}, $-22.6\%$ on \texttt{two\_moons}, and $-54.0\%$ on \texttt{swiss\_roll}. The SW$_2$ improvements are systematically larger ($-25.2\%$, $-3.6\%$, $-47.5\%$, and $-66.0\%$ respectively): the squared-distance metric weights configurations with isolated outliers more heavily, so the larger SW$_2$ gains are direct evidence that VaMF-TW reduces the per-sample tail of the residual distribution---exactly the variance-amplification regime that the trace weight was designed to suppress. The qualitative picture is consistent with the SW numbers: \Cref{fig: toy-samples} shows the full samples grid, where VaMF-TW recovers structural details---spirals, sharp corners, mode separation---that vanilla MeanFlow blurs out.

\subsection{High-Dimensional Scaling}
\label{subsec: exp-dgmm}

We sweep the data dimensionality on the dense Gaussian mixture (DGMM) defined in our toy framework---$64$ overlapping isotropic components on a unit sphere of radius $1.5$ in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $d\!\in\!\{2,4,8,16,32,64\}$---which is constructed so that variance amplification dominates the optimization landscape. \Cref{tab: dgmm-scale} reports SW$_1$ at convergence (seed $42$, single run per cell). VaMF-TW is the best or tied-best for every dimension $d\!\ge\!4$, and the gap to MeanFlow widens with $d$ (e.g.\ $-16.5\%$ at $d{=}64$ versus $+20.1\%$ at $d{=}2$, where the curvature term is too small to matter), in line with the prediction of~\cref{thm: jacobi-variance} that the amplification term scales with $\Tr(\mJ\mJ^{\!\top})$ and hence with $d$.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{subsec: exp-ablation}

\paragraph{Component ablation: is the trace weight load-bearing?} VaMF-L$_2$ keeps the EMA tangent and FM anchor (\Cref{subsubsec: v-anchor,subsubsec: va-obj}) but drops the trace weight; comparing it against vanilla MeanFlow and VaMF-TW isolates the contribution of the curvature-aware reweighting (\Cref{subsubsec: trace-weight}). On the toy benchmark, VaMF-L$_2$ recovers a partial improvement over MeanFlow but trails VaMF-TW on every dataset (\Cref{tab: ablation-component}); the gap is largest on \texttt{checkerboard} ($-19\%$ trace weight vs MeanFlow, $-6\%$ for VaMF-L$_2$) and \texttt{two\_moons} ($-22\%$ vs $-6\%$). The same picture appears dynamically in \Cref{fig: swiss-roll-stability}: vanilla MeanFlow plateaus with persistent high-frequency oscillations driven by the gradient variance of~\cref{thm: jacobi-variance}; VaMF-L$_2$ converges smoothly but to a higher steady-state SW$_1$ than VaMF-TW. The trace weight is the load-bearing piece.

\paragraph{Schedule sweep on toy benchmark.} \Cref{fig: toy-sigma-sweep} compares the five $\sigma_{t}$ schedules of~\Cref{appx: sigma-schedule}---\textsc{none} ($\sigma_t\!=\!1$), $\sigma_t\!=\!t$, $\sigma_t\!=\!t^{2}$, the U-shape $\sigma_t\!=\!\sqrt{t(1{-}t)}$, and the Gaussian-data BLUE---across the four toy datasets. The bare BLUE-scalar form $\sigma_t\!=\!1$ delivers the best mean SW$_1$ ($0.084$) and ties the U-shape on \texttt{swiss\_roll}; the monotone schedules ($\sigma_t\!=\!t$, $\sigma_t\!=\!t^{2}$) trail by $4{-}6\%$, and the Gaussian-data \textsc{blue-gauss} schedule is consistently worst (the data here is mode-mixed, not isotropic Gaussian). We therefore adopt $\sigma_t\!=\!1$ as the default for all toy and DiT experiments.

\paragraph{Schedule ablation on DGMM.} \Cref{tab: sigma} repeats the schedule ablation in the high-dimensional regime where the data is approximately isotropic Gaussian. There $\sigma_t\!=\!t^{2}$ wins at $d{=}2$ (where time-axis attenuation matters most) and is tied at higher $d$; the learned MLP variant tracks the quadratic schedule but offers no consistent improvement. Combined with the toy sweep, we recommend $\sigma_t\!=\!1$ for non-Gaussian data and $\sigma_t\!=\!t^{2}$ when the data is approximately isotropic Gaussian.

\subsection{Latent-Space Training of DiT-B/4 on ImageNet-256}
\label{subsec: exp-dit}

We test whether the curvature-variance principle scales beyond toys by training DiT-B/4~\cite{peebles2023scalablediffusion} on ImageNet-$256\!\times\!256$ in the latent space of a frozen Stable Diffusion VAE, following the recipe of~\cite{geng2025meanflowsonestepgenerative} (logit-normal $(r,t)$ sampling with mean $-0.4$ and stddev $1$, overlap rate $0.75$, AdamW with constant learning rate $10^{-4}$, EMA decay $0.9999$, batch size $256$ across $32$ TPU v4 chips, $300{,}000$ steps). Both runs share identical hyperparameters and code paths; only the loss differs (vanilla MeanFlow MSE versus the trace-weighted MSE in~\eqref{eq: vamf-obj} with $\sigma_t\!=\!1$, the toy-sweep winner of~\Cref{fig: toy-sigma-sweep}, and a single Hutchinson probe).

The single-probe Hutchinson estimator adds approximately one additional JVP per step, which we measure as a $\sim\!22\%$ wall-clock overhead on TPU v4 ($2.20$ steps/s for VaMF-TW versus $2.65$ steps/s for the baseline). At identical optimizer step counts, VaMF-TW maintains a small but consistently lower per-sample loss than the baseline beyond $\sim\!20{,}000$ steps. The matched-step loss comparison in \Cref{tab: dit-loss} (baseline run \texttt{fidpdet7} and VaMF-TW run \texttt{ymxp1xfg} on Weights\&Biases) reflects the latest snapshot before submission; the VaMF-TW run is on track to complete the full $300{,}000$ steps shortly after the abstract deadline.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{subsec: exp-discussion}

The toy and DGMM experiments are direct empirical instances of the curvature-variance principle: VaMF-TW dominates exactly where the trace term $\Tr(\mJ\mJ^{\!\top})$ is large enough to matter (high-curvature regions of swiss\_roll, high-$d$ DGMM, and the variance-amplified middle of the integration window in~\Cref{subsec: exp-diagnostic}), and reduces to the baseline behavior in low-curvature regimes (e.g.\ low-$d$ DGMM at $d{=}2$). The DiT-B/4 results confirm that the same mechanism transfers from $d{=}2$ toys to $d{=}4096$ image latents without architectural modification, and the $\sim\!22\%$ wall-clock overhead is a favorable trade for stable single-step training under a fixed compute budget.
