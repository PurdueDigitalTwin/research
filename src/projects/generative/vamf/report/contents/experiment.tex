\section{Empirical Validation of the Control-Variate Framework}
\label{sec: experiment}

We empirically validate the framework of~\Cref{sec: method} through a direct probe of the bias-variance landscape $M(\beta)$: rather than picking a single instantiation and benchmarking it against vanilla MeanFlow, we sweep the tangent-mixing coefficient $\beta\!\in\![0,1]$ end-to-end and compare the empirical landscape to the closed-form prediction of~\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum}. This isolates the framework's predictions from confounds of any single training recipe.

\paragraph{Setup.} The interior-$\beta$ instantiation~\eqref{eq: tangent-mix} interpolates between vanilla MeanFlow ($\beta\!=\!0$, conditional-velocity tangent) and the EMA-tangent corner ($\beta\!=\!1$). For 2-D toy benchmarks we use a $3$-layer MLP with $128$ hidden units, $200{,}000$ optimization steps, three random seeds $\{42, 0, 1\}$, and one shared backbone, sampling schedule, and optimizer per dataset; only $\beta$ varies. We measure (i)~the per-step gradient covariance $\Tr(\Cov[\nabla_{\vtheta}\ell_{\text{MF}}])$ from $K\!=\!8$ replica mini-batches (\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}) on a $4$-dataset, single-seed sub-sweep at $\beta\!\in\!\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ (gradient-variance probing has non-trivial overhead), and (ii)~the final-step sliced Wasserstein-$1$ (sample quality) on the full $\{0.0,0.1,\dots,1.0\}$ grid, evaluated on $4096$ samples with $500$ random projections under a fixed projection key per evaluation for variance-reduced paired estimates.

\subsection{Variance reduction matches~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}}
\label{subsec: exp-grad-var}

\Cref{fig: nr-vs-beta} reports $\Tr(\Cov[\nabla_{\vtheta}\ell_{\text{MF}}])(\beta)$ across the gradient-variance sub-sweep on four 2-D toy datasets, averaged over the second half of training to suppress transient. The relation is monotone decreasing in $\beta$ on every dataset, with a $1.7\!\times\!\!\sim\!\!4.7\!\times$ reduction at $\beta\!=\!1$ relative to vanilla MeanFlow (per-dataset ratios in~\Cref{fig: nr-vs-beta}). The largest reduction ($4.71\!\times$) appears on \texttt{eight\_gaussians}, where the trained backbone has the largest spectral magnitude $\|\mJ\|$; the smallest ($1.73\!\times$) on \texttt{swiss\_roll}, where the backbone is closer to the no-amplification regime $\partial_{\vz}\vu_{\vtheta}\!\approx\!\frac{1}{t-r}\mI_{d}$. The empirical ordering tracks the variance term $\sigma^{2}d\bigl((1{-}\beta)\kappa - \beta\bigr)^{2}$ from~\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum} and~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}, providing direct empirical evidence that the conditional-tangent fluctuation $\vv'$ is the dominant variance source at the gradient level.

\subsection{The empirical $M(\beta)$ landscape}
\label{subsec: exp-m-beta}

Whereas $\Tr(\Cov[\nabla_{\vtheta}\ell_{\text{MF}}])(\beta)$ probes the variance term in isolation, the sample-quality landscape $\mathrm{SW}_{1}(\beta)$ exposes the full bias-variance trade-off. \Cref{fig: sw1-vs-beta} traces $\mathrm{SW}_{1}(\beta)$ across the full $11$-point grid on six 2-D toy datasets ($3$ seeds, error bars are SEM). On the five high-curvature datasets (\texttt{checkerboard}, \texttt{eight\_gaussians}, \texttt{two\_moons}, \texttt{swiss\_roll}, \texttt{pinwheel}), the empirical $\beta^{\ast}$ falls in $\{0.8,1.0\}$, recovering the predicted near-corner location for the small-$\|\vb\|^{2}$ regime where the EMA proxy is accurate (the James-Stein shrinkage factor $\sigma^{2}d/(\sigma^{2}d{+}\|\vb\|^{2})$ approaches $1$). On the lower-curvature \texttt{two\_spirals} dataset, the trained backbone produces $\partial_{\vz}\vu_{\vtheta}$ close to $\frac{1}{t-r}\mI_{d}$, driving $\kappa$ toward zero. The predicted $\beta^{\ast}$ then shrinks toward the unbiased corner $\beta\!=\!0$ through the noise-cancellation factor $\kappa/(\kappa{+}1)$ in~\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum}, and the empirical sweep concurs ($\beta^{\ast}\!=\!0$). On \texttt{swiss\_roll} the SW$_{1}$ reduction at the empirical $\beta^{\ast}\!=\!1$ relative to $\beta\!=\!0$ is $51\%$ ($0.098\!\to\!0.048$), and a parallel matched-method comparison of vanilla MeanFlow against the full $\beta\!=\!1$ corner recipe (EMA tangent + FM anchor; \Cref{tab: toy}) reaches $54\%$.

\subsection{High-dimensional scaling on DGMM}
\label{subsec: exp-dgmm}

We sweep $\beta\!\in\!\{0,0.1,\dots,1\}$ on a dense Gaussian mixture (DGMM) in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $d\!\in\!\{2,4,8,16,32,64\}$ (three seeds $\{42,0,1\}$, $200$k steps, otherwise identical setup to the 2-D toys; error bars are SEM). \Cref{fig: dgmm-beta} reports $\mathrm{SW}_{1}(\beta)$ per dimension. Two regimes emerge cleanly under the seed-aggregated mean: at $d\!\in\!\{2,4,8,16\}$ the empirical $\beta^{\ast}$ sits at or near the deterministic-tangent corner ($\beta^{\ast}\!\in\!\{0.8,1.0\}$), while at $d\!\in\!\{32,64\}$ the optimum moves into the interior ($\beta^{\ast}\!=\!0.1$ at $d\!=\!32$, $\beta^{\ast}\!=\!0.4$ at $d\!=\!64$). The transition is consistent with~\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum}: at low $d$ the EMA proxy is highly accurate ($\|\vb\|^{2}$ small, shrinkage $\sigma^{2}d/(\sigma^{2}d{+}\|\vb\|^{2})\!\approx\!1$) and $\kappa/(\kappa{+}1)$ saturates near $1$; at higher $d$ the network's Jacobian spectrum drifts off saturation, $\kappa$ drops, and the noise-cancellation factor $\kappa/(\kappa{+}1)$ pulls $\beta^{\ast}$ to the interior. The crossover signature is a direct empirical fingerprint of the two-factor decomposition.

\subsection{Validation at DiT-B/4 / ImageNet-$256$ scale}
\label{subsec: exp-dit}

To check that the framework's predictions hold beyond the toy regime, we run a four-point sweep $\beta\!\in\!\{0, 0.25, 0.5, 1\}$ on a class-conditional DiT-B/4~\cite{peebles2023scalablediffusion} trained on Stable Diffusion VAE latents ($32{\times}32{\times}4$, $d\!=\!4096$); see~\Cref{appx: dit-results} for the full setup, FID table, diagnostic measurements, and DiT-specific bias and shrinkage trajectory.

\Cref{fig: dit-fid-curves} bundles two complementary measurements at DiT scale. \emph{Panel (a)} confirms that the gradient-variance reduction predicted by~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance} carries over from toys to DiT. On the same trained checkpoint (baseline, step $80$k), we measure the per-step loss variance under two tangent choices: the stochastic conditional-velocity tangent used during baseline training ($\beta\!=\!0$ corner) and the deterministic EMA tangent ($\beta\!=\!1$ corner). The deterministic-tangent variance stays roughly flat across $t$ (range $\sim\!17\text{–}55\!$k), while the stochastic-tangent variance grows by two orders of magnitude — from $\sim\!21\!$k at $t\!=\!0.1$ to $\sim\!2.2\!$M at $t\!=\!0.9$. The widening gap is exactly the Jacobi-factor amplification of~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}: the stochastic tangent passes the conditional-velocity noise through $\mJ\!=\!(t{-}r)\partial_{\vz}\vu{-}\mI$, which the deterministic tangent eliminates by construction. The same pattern holds on the $\beta\!=\!1$ checkpoint (Appendix~\ref{appx: dit-results}), confirming that the amplification is a property of the architecture and data, not of the training regime. \emph{Panel (b)} visualizes the converged-FID landscape as a function of $\beta$, with empirical $\Delta\mathrm{FID}(\beta)\!\triangleq\!\mathrm{FID}(\beta)\!-\!\mathrm{FID}_{\text{baseline}}$ (each run's last logged FID at step $295$k) overlaid against the MSE-prediction curve $\beta^{2}\!\cdot\!A$ (anchored at $\beta\!=\!0.5$). The four-point matched-step ordering $\mathrm{FID}(\beta\!=\!0)\!<\!\mathrm{FID}(\beta\!=\!0.25)\!<\!\mathrm{FID}(\beta\!=\!0.5)\!<\!\mathrm{FID}(\beta\!=\!1)$ holds at \emph{every} $5$k-aligned matched-step checkpoint after the early-training transient ($54$ consecutive datapoints across all four runs from step $30$k to step $295$k); per-checkpoint values are reported in~\Cref{tab: dit-fid}.

\paragraph{FID-vs-MSE landscape mismatch.} Direct measurement of both ingredients of~\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum} on the DiT checkpoints (EMA-tracking proxy $\widehat{\|\vb\|^{2}}$, irreducible noise floor $\sigma^{2}d$, both reported in~\Cref{appx: dit-results}) places the formula-predicted optimum in the interior range $\beta^{\ast}\!\in\![0.46,\,0.50]$ under the scalar-isotropic approximation; the directly-probed matrix-form $\beta^{\ast}_{\text{matrix}}$ is even larger (see below). The empirical $\beta\!=\!0.5$-beats-$\beta\!=\!1$ ordering at convergence is consistent with that prediction. To test whether FID tracks the gradient-MSE landscape \emph{quantitatively}, we anchor the MSE-prediction curve $\beta^{2}\!\cdot\!A$ (with $A\!=\!\Delta\mathrm{FID}(\beta\!=\!0.5)/0.25$) at the $\beta\!=\!0.5$ point and compare the $\beta\!=\!1$ offset. Using the matched-step ($295$k) FIDs, $\Delta\mathrm{FID}(\beta\!=\!0.5)\!\approx\!1.14$ and $\Delta\mathrm{FID}(\beta\!=\!1)\!\approx\!11.99$ from the baseline floor $\mathrm{FID}_{\text{baseline}}\!=\!11.37$, while the MSE prediction at $\beta\!=\!1$ is only $4.58$ — a $\sim\!2.6\!\times$ super-linear amplification on the FID axis (\Cref{fig: dit-fid-curves}). The FID landscape therefore over-penalizes bias relative to gradient MSE. As a consequence, the FID-optimal $\beta$ shifts past the gradient-MSE interior minimizer all the way to the unbiased corner $\beta\!=\!0$.

\paragraph{Direct matrix-form measurement.} To verify that the scalar-isotropic prediction is not artifactual, we directly measured a matrix-form upper bound on $\beta^{\ast}_{\text{matrix}}$ of~\Cref{appx: theorem-proof} on the baseline checkpoint by sampling actual $\vv'$ values from the conditional distribution (no Hutchinson probes; we average $(\mJ\vv')\!\cdot\!((\mJ{+}\mI)\vv')$ and $\|(\mJ{+}\mI)\vv'\|^{2}$ across data samples, which is unbiased for the trace formulas regardless of how anisotropic $\Sigma_{\vv'}$ is). The estimator omits the bias term $\|(\mJ{+}\mI)\vb\|^{2}$ from the denominator, giving
\begin{equation*}
    \beta^{\ast}_{\text{no bias}}\!\triangleq\!\Tr(\mJ\Sigma_{\vv'}(\mJ{+}\mI)^{\!\top})/\Tr((\mJ{+}\mI)\Sigma_{\vv'}(\mJ{+}\mI)^{\!\top})\;\geq\;|\beta^{\ast}_{\text{matrix}}|,
\end{equation*}
since the omitted bias term is non-negative. We probe at $t\!\in\!\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9\}$, fixed gap $t-r\!=\!0.25$, $512$ samples per $t$ (using the model's own boundary $\vu_{\vtheta}(\vx_{t},t,t)$ as the marginal-velocity proxy, asymptotically correct at $\beta\!=\!0$ training convergence). The numerator is \emph{strictly positive} at every probed $t$, ruling out the unbiased-corner regime $\beta^{\ast}_{\text{matrix}}\!\le\!0$. Aggregated across $t$, $\beta^{\ast}_{\text{no bias}}\!\approx\!0.94$ — close to the deterministic-tangent corner and \emph{larger} than the scalar-isotropic estimate $\beta^{\ast}\!\in\![0.46,0.50]$. The full $\beta^{\ast}_{\text{matrix}}$ then lies in $(0,0.94]$; we do not pin down the exact value because a clean estimator of the true model bias $\vb\!=\!\vu_{\vtheta}(\vx_{t},t,t)-\vv(\vx_{t},t)$ is intractable on ImageNet without access to the conditional distribution $p(\vx_{0}\!\mid\!\vx_{t})$, and substituting the EMA copy $\vu_{\bar{\vtheta}}$ for $\vv$ would only measure an EMA-tracking gap rather than the true bias. The matrix-form measurement therefore strengthens, rather than weakens, the FID-vs-MSE mismatch: the gradient-MSE optimum sits in $(0,0.94]$ while the FID optimum sits at the unbiased corner $\beta\!=\!0$ — the strict interior-vs-corner gap on the gradient-MSE axis is preserved under the matrix-form analysis.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{subsec: exp-discussion}

The full-sweep validation establishes three points jointly. \emph{(i)~Variance scaling}: $\Tr(\Cov[\nabla_{\vtheta}\ell_{\text{MF}}])(\beta)$ matches the variance term $\sigma^{2}d\bigl((1{-}\beta)\kappa-\beta\bigr)^{2}$ of~\Cref{thm: control-variate-optimum} on every dataset (\Cref{fig: nr-vs-beta}), with $1.7\!\times\!\!\sim\!\!4.7\!\times$ reduction at $\beta\!=\!1$. \Cref{thm: jacobi-variance} identifies the same Jacobi-amplified term as the variance source at the special case $\beta\!=\!0$. \emph{(ii)~Bias-variance landscape}: the empirical $\mathrm{SW}_{1}(\beta)$ landscape recovers the predicted shape on six 2-D toy datasets (\Cref{fig: sw1-vs-beta}); the empirical $\beta^{\ast}$ tracks the closed-form prediction within seed standard error, including the corner shrinkage to $\beta^{\ast}\!=\!0$ on \texttt{two\_spirals} where $\kappa$ is small. \emph{(iii)~Scale invariance}: at DiT-B/4 / ImageNet-$256$ ($d\!=\!4096$) the predicted ordering across $\beta\!\in\!\{0, 0.25, 0.5, 1\}$ holds at every matched-step checkpoint (\Cref{fig: dit-fid-curves}), and the empirical asymptotic offsets reveal a quantitative super-linear FID dependence on the gradient-MSE-axis bias term — corroborated by direct matrix-form measurement of $\beta^{\ast}\!\approx\!0.94$ on the gradient-MSE axis versus the FID-optimum at $\beta\!=\!0$. Together these establish that the control-variate framework correctly identifies both the gradient-variance source in MeanFlow training and the bias-variance ordering at the loss-landscape level, across four orders of magnitude in dimensionality.

The framework also recasts a family of concurrent works (iMF~\cite{geng2025improvedmeanflowschallenges}, AlphaFlow~\cite{zhang2025alphaflowunderstandingimprovingmeanflow}, Re-MeanFlow~\cite{zhang2026overcomingcurvaturebottleneckmeanflow}, TVM~\cite{zhou2026terminalvelocitymatching}) as practical realizations of the same one-parameter optimum at differing $\beta$, $\kappa$, or $\|\vb\|^{2}$ regimes (\Cref{tab: concurrent-positioning}). Our $\beta\!=\!0.5$ DiT measurement shows that pushing $\beta\!\to\!1$ pays a quantifiable FID cost beyond what the gradient-MSE picture predicts. This observation, novel to this work, suggests that practical $\beta$ should be chosen jointly with the downstream evaluation metric rather than uniformly at the gradient-MSE optimum.
