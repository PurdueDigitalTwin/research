\section{Introduction}
\label{sec: introduction}

Flow-based generative models~\cite{chen2018neural,rezende2015variational,dinh2017density,grathwohl2019ffjord,lipman2023flowmatchinggenerativemodeling,tong2024improvinggeneralizingflowbasedgenerative,albergo2023buildingnormalizingflowsstochastic} learn continuous-time transformations between a simple prior and a target data distribution. Recent conditional flow matching~\cite{lipman2023flowmatchinggenerativemodeling,tong2024improvinggeneralizingflowbasedgenerative} and stochastic interpolant~\cite{albergo2023buildingnormalizingflowsstochastic,albergo2025stochasticinterpolants} frameworks enable simulation-free training by regressing a velocity field, achieving sample quality competitive with diffusion models~\cite{sohldickstein2015deep,song2019generative,ho2020denoising,nichol2021improved,song2021scorebased,dhariwal2021diffusion,karras2022elucidating} and variational autoencoders~\cite{kingma2014auto}. Like diffusion models, however, they require iterative integration at inference time, motivating methods that reduce the number of function evaluations to one or few steps~\cite{song2021denoising}. Existing approaches include progressive distillation~\cite{salimans2022progressivedistillationfastsampling}, distribution matching~\cite{yin2024distributionmatchingdistillation,yin2024improveddistributionmatchingdistillation}, consistency models~\cite{song2023consistencymodels,lu2025simplifyingstabilizingscalingcontinuoustime}, and flow map matching~\cite{boffi2025flowmapmatchingstochastic}, but most require a pre-trained teacher model.

MeanFlow~\cite{geng2025meanflowsonestepgenerative} offers an appealing \textit{distillation-free} alternative by learning a two-parameter average velocity field $\vu(\rvx,r,t)$ through a total-derivative identity. In principle, this identity provides exact self-supervision: the compound prediction $\vu+(t-r)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\vu$ should match the instantaneous velocity at convergence, requiring no external teacher. In practice, however, MeanFlow training is plagued by non-decreasing loss and prohibitively high gradient variance~\cite{zhang2025alphaflowunderstandingimprovingmeanflow,geng2025improvedmeanflowschallenges}. Concurrent works diagnose this from different angles: AlphaFlow~\cite{zhang2025alphaflowunderstandingimprovingmeanflow} identifies gradient conflict between loss components; Re-MeanFlow~\cite{zhang2026overcomingcurvaturebottleneckmeanflow} attributes instability to trajectory curvature; and Improved MeanFlow~\cite{geng2025improvedmeanflowschallenges} traces it to Jacobian amplification in the JVP. Each proposes a different fix---$\alpha$-scheduling, trajectory rectification, and a separate velocity head---treating the problems as isolated.

We show that these are \textit{three manifestations of a single quantity}: the material derivative $\frac{\mathrm{D}\vv}{\mathrm{D}t}:=\partial_{t}\vv+(\nabla_{\rvx}\vv)\vv$ of the marginal velocity field, which measures how rapidly the velocity changes along its own trajectories. We call this the \textbf{curvature-variance principle}. Specifically, we prove that: \textbf{(i)} the curvature gap $\Delta:=\vu-\vv$ satisfies $\Delta\approx-\frac{(t-r)}{2}\frac{\mathrm{D}\vv}{\mathrm{D}t}$, so it is the local flow acceleration that causes the average and instantaneous velocities to diverge; \textbf{(ii)} the per-step gradient variance is amplified by a Jacobi factor $\Tr(\mJ\Sigma_{\vv'}\mJ^{\!\top})$ where $\mJ=(t-r)\partial_{\vz}\vu_{\vtheta}-\mI_{d}$, and the stop-gradient operator creates a semi-gradient gap that blinds the optimizer to this amplification; and \textbf{(iii)} the acceleration-to-noise ratio $\text{ANR}=(t-r)\|\frac{\mathrm{D}\vv}{\mathrm{D}t}\|/\sqrt{\Tr(\Sigma_{\vv'})}$ governs the optimal sampling distribution over intervals, connecting scheduling heuristics in prior work to a principled criterion.

From this analysis we derive \textbf{Variance-Aware MeanFlow (VaMF)}, a training framework that addresses all identified failure modes without architectural modification. VaMF combines: (1)~an \textit{EMA velocity anchor} that provides a deterministic JVP tangent, eliminating Jacobian variance amplification; (2)~a \textit{flow matching anchor} that supervises the boundary condition, preventing tangent bias without a separate network head; and (3)~a \textit{heteroscedastic variance head} that learns per-sample weighting, automatically subsuming inverse-variance weighting, progressive scheduling, and acceleration-aware interval sampling. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A theoretical framework that unifies gradient conflict, Jacobian amplification, and the curvature bottleneck under the curvature-variance principle, including the first formal quantification of the MeanFlow semi-gradient gap (\cref{subsec: mf-bias-vars,subsec: flow-acc}).
    \item The VA-MeanFlow training framework that eliminates variance amplification and tangent bias through EMA anchoring, FM anchoring, and a learned variance head---without modifying the backbone (\cref{subsec: vamf}).
    \item A formal comparison between the $\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t$ and $\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}s$ formulations, revealing the tradeoff between tangent estimation error and architectural compatibility (\Cref{appx: additional-results}).
\end{itemize}
