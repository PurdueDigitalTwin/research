\begin{abstract}
    \label{sec: abstract}
    Mean Flows enables distillation-free one-step generation through learning two-parameter average velocity fields from noise to data distributions, but their training is notorious for non-decreasing loss and high gradient variances. We establish a theoretical framework to understand the phenomenon through the lens of \textit{variance reduction with a control variate}. We identify two distinct roles for the use of conditional velocity field $\vv_{\text{cond}}$ in training, both as an unbiased target and as the Monte Carlo control variate for the tangent in Jacobi-vector-product (JVP) but with an inappropriate coefficient. The latter inflates per-step gradient variance and contribute to the non-decreasing loss. We further derive the optimal tangent-mixing coefficient $\beta^{\ast}$ in closed form and unify concurrent works by showing that each of them corresponds to a different practical realization of the optimum. Emprically, the derived optimal coefficient yields $3\!\times\!\sim\!5\!\times$ gradient-variance reduction with up to $54\%$ improvements in sliced Wasserstein-$1$ distance. We reproduce the same control-variate signature on latent diffusion Transformers, with $\approx\!5\!\times$ reduction in gradient noise ratio in the dominant training regime.
\end{abstract}
