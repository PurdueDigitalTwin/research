\begin{abstract}
    \label{sec: abstract}
    Mean Flows promise distillation-free, one-step generation by optimizing an identity between the average and instantaneous velocity field. In practice they suffer from a non-decreasing, high-variance loss whose root cause has been diagnosed by concurrent work as gradient conflict, Jacobian amplification, and a curvature bottleneck. We show that all three diagnoses are manifestations of a single quantity---the material derivative of the marginal velocity field---and call this the \textit{curvature-variance principle}. From this principle we derive \textit{Variance-Aware MeanFlow} (VaMF), a backbone-preserving training framework that combines an EMA velocity anchor (deterministic JVP tangent), a flow-matching anchor (boundary supervision), and a Hutchinson-estimated trace weight that downweights each sample by the local Jacobian-deviation norm $\Tr(\mJ\mJ^{\!\top})$---a closed-form scalar approximation to the BLUE per-sample weight under isotropic conditional-velocity noise. Empirically, VaMF improves the 2-D toy benchmark sliced Wasserstein-$1$ over vanilla MeanFlow by $-23\%$, $-13\%$, $-23\%$, and $-54\%$ on \texttt{checkerboard}, \texttt{eight\_gaussians}, \texttt{two\_moons}, and \texttt{swiss\_roll} respectively (mean $-28\%$ over $3$ seeds), dominates from $d{=}4$ up to $d{=}64$ on the dense-Gaussian-mixture scaling sweep, and transfers to DiT-B/4 / ImageNet-$256$ latent-space training at a $\sim\!22\%$ wall-clock overhead per step.
\end{abstract}
