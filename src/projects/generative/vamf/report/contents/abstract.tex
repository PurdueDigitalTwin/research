\begin{abstract}
    \label{sec: abstract}
    Mean Flows learn a two-parameter average velocity field that enables distillation-free one-step generation, but their training is notorious for non-decreasing loss and high gradient variance. Concurrent work proposes a variety of practical fixes---deterministic tangents from EMA teachers, separate velocity heads, terminal-time differentiation, reflow preprocessing---each effective in isolation but lacking a unifying explanation. We provide that explanation through the lens of \emph{variance reduction with a control variate}. We show that the conditional velocity $\vv_{\text{cond}}$ plays two distinct statistical roles in the MeanFlow loss: as the regression target it is an unbiased estimator of the marginal velocity, but as the JVP tangent it is a Monte Carlo control variate with the wrong coefficient. The latter inflates per-step gradient variance by a factor $\boldsymbol{g}^{\!\top}\!\mJ\Sigma_{\vv'}\mJ^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{g}$ that scales unboundedly with the spatial Jacobian magnitude. We derive the optimal tangent-mixing coefficient $\beta^{\star}$ in closed form, prove that target-replacement biases are uniformly $\gO(\|\vb\|)$ while tangent-replacement biases are gap-attenuated $\gO((t{-}r)\|\vb\|)$ (justifying the asymmetric design adopted by every concurrent fix), and show that every concurrent method corresponds to a different practical realization of the optimum---formally unifying them under one principle. Empirically, on six $2$-D toy benchmarks the optimal coefficient yields $3\!\times\!\!\sim\!5\!\times$ gradient-variance reduction with up to $-54\%$ SW$_{1}$ improvements; at DiT-B/4 ImageNet-$256$ scale ($d{=}4096$) the same control-variate signature reproduces, with $\sim\!5\!\times$ reduction in gradient noise ratio in the dominant training regime. Both extremes ($\beta\!=\!0$ vanilla, $\beta\!=\!1$ deterministic-tangent) are strict corners of the bias-variance trade-off, with the predicted interior optimum dominating both.
\end{abstract}
