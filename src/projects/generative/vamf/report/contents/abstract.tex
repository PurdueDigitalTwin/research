\begin{abstract}
    \label{sec: abstract}
    Mean Flows enable distillation-free one-step generation by learning two-parameter average-velocity fields from noise to data distributions, but their training is notorious for non-decreasing loss and high gradient variance. We establish a theoretical framework to understand the phenomenon through the lens of \textit{variance reduction with a control variate}. We identify two distinct roles for the conditional velocity field $\vv_{\text{cond}}$ in training: as an unbiased target and as the Monte Carlo control variate for the tangent in the Jacobi-vector-product (JVP), but with an inappropriate coefficient. The latter inflates per-step gradient variance and contributes to the non-decreasing loss. We further derive the optimal tangent-mixing coefficient $\beta^{\ast}$ in closed form, unify the concurrent works by showing them as different practical realizations of the optimum, and propose a plug-and-play training framework, \textit{Variance-Aware MeanFlow} (VaMF), to achieve the optimum with an EMA-derived proxy and a flow-matching anchor. Empirically, we find that a better coefficient yields $3\!\times\!\sim\!5\!\times$ gradient-variance reduction with up to $54\%$ improvements in sliced Wasserstein-$1$ distance. We reproduce the same control-variate signature on latent diffusion Transformers, with $\approx\!5\!\times$ reduction in gradient noise ratio in the dominant training regime.
\end{abstract}
