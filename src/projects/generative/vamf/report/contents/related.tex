\section{Related Work}
\label{sec: review}

\paragraph{MeanFlow and its training pathology.} Mean Flows~\cite{geng2025meanflowsonestepgenerative} learn an average velocity field via a total-derivative identity, enabling distillation-free one-step generation. The empirical pathology, non-decreasing loss with high gradient variance, was diagnosed in concurrent work from several angles. AlphaFlow~\cite{zhang2025alphaflowunderstandingimprovingmeanflow} attributes slow convergence to gradient conflict between flow-matching and total-derivative consistency components and proposes an $\alpha$-curriculum. Improved MeanFlow~\cite{geng2025improvedmeanflowschallenges} replaces the conditional-velocity tangent with a separate marginal-velocity head trained jointly with stop-gradient. Kim~\textit{et al.}~\cite{kim2025understandingacceleratingmeanflow} dissect the interaction between instantaneous and average velocity fields and propose a staged training scheme that accelerates convergence. Modular MeanFlow~\cite{you2025modularmeanflow} derives a family of MeanFlow losses from the differential identity and adds gradient modulation for stability. Re-MeanFlow~\cite{zhang2026overcomingcurvaturebottleneckmeanflow} addresses trajectory curvature through reflow pre-processing that straightens conditional paths. Decoupled MeanFlow~\cite{lee2025decoupledmeanflow} converts a pretrained flow model into a flow map by conditioning the final blocks on a subsequent timestep, sidestepping joint training of the average velocity. Terminal Velocity Matching (TVM)~\cite{zhou2026terminalvelocitymatching} bypasses the spatial Jacobian by differentiating with respect to the terminal time. Functional Mean Flows~\cite{li2025functionalmeanflowhilbert} extends MeanFlow to Hilbert spaces; Riemannian MeanFlow~\cite{woo2026riemannianmeanflow} extends it to manifolds; and OT-MeanFlow~\cite{akbari2025otmeanflow3d} couples the construction with optimal transport for $3$D point-cloud generation. Each of these works proposes an effective practical fix or domain extension; in contrast, our work isolates the statistical role of the JVP tangent itself, identifies the conditional fluctuation $\vv'$ as a control variate with the wrong coefficient inside a semi-gradient JVP, and derives the optimal coefficient. Concurrent fixes that act on the tangent or proxy correspond to different practical realizations of this optimum (\Cref{tab: concurrent-positioning}); curvature-reduction methods (Re-MeanFlow) instead shrink $\|\mJ\|$ in our framework, which lowers the amplification factor by~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance}.

\paragraph{Variance reduction in flow training.} Several recent works address variance in flow-based training from a complementary angle. Stable Velocity~\cite{yang2026stablevelocityvarianceperspective} reveals a two-regime variance structure in flow matching and proposes composite conditioning; TPC~\cite{maduabuchi2026temporalpairconsistencyvariancereduced} reduces variance through temporal pair coupling; and preconditioned flow matching~\cite{ahamed2026preconditionedscoreflowmatching} addresses ill-conditioning via anisotropic preconditioning. Bertrand et al.~\cite{bertrand2025closedformflowmatchinggeneralization} argue that stochastic-target variance is negligible for standard flow matching, consistent with our analysis, since the problematic amplification we identify is specific to the MeanFlow JVP, not the flow-matching loss itself. Rectified flows~\cite{liu2022flowstraightfastlearning,lee2024improvingrectifiedflows} reduce trajectory curvature by iterative straightening, which by~\Cref{thm: jacobi-variance} reduces $\|\mJ\|^{2}$ and hence the amplified noise term.

\paragraph{Control variates and target networks.} The control-variate framework we apply has classical roots in Monte Carlo estimation~\cite{glynn2002some}; it is also the analytical structure underlying target networks in deep RL~\cite{mnih2015human} and self-distillation in self-supervised learning~\cite{grill2020bootstrap}. We make the connection explicit by interpreting the EMA-derived tangent in MeanFlow as a target-network-style proxy that drives the control-variate coefficient toward its optimum.
